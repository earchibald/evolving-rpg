## findings

**1. The only thing said all run was said from outside the world, and the verb was "pass."** — "looks like the monsters on this level are very very very difficult to pass with the new changes". Not *beat*, not *fight* — **pass**. `spoke: false`, `takes: []`: 273 turns produced no spoken word at all, only this one typed line about the build. That is a player evaluating a patch, not inhabiting a floor. And it lands exactly on today's change (`docs/design/BALANCE.md`, "The teaching floor reaches the player"), which put depth 1's keen edge **and its guard** on the start→exit walk, eight steps in. The intent was to arm the player early. What arrived was a toll gate. The mechanism is placement: the only power on the floor sits behind the fight it exists to win.

**2. They died bare — again — while carrying something they never spent.** Finished `0 of 10 hit points, might 3, speed 4, wits 3` — untouched starting stats — with `picked up 1 things`. Walking over a relic auto-takes it when it dominates empty gear (`src/core/commands.ts:1013`), so might 3 proves the edge was never reached; the one pickup was an off-path satchel provision, and `1 were still standing` says the guard outlived them. So the player detoured for the provision the design says "pays for scouting," carried it to 0 hit points, and never used it. Two failures in one line: the on-path power was unreachable, and the power they *did* hold had nothing telling them to spend it.

**3. The retry was byte-identical, and that is the deepest wound.** Two submissions from world "main" this hour — 65 turns at 04:01, 273 turns at 04:38. The combat ledger does not differ by a digit: `swung 6 times, landing 2, dealing 8 damage` / `swung at 6 times, hit 4 times, taking 12 damage` / `1 creatures died; 1 were still standing`. Between the two the player took **231 more steps** and walked into walls 4 times. Everything they *did* changed; nothing that *happened* did. That is the purest form of "no decision to make": the second life on the same seed replays the first fight exactly, so the only variable left is how far you walk before it happens again.

**4. The player held still **6 times** in the 65-turn life and **2 times** in the 273-turn one, and `No rule fired during this run` both times. The mechanism is geometric, not statistical: `nothing living within 6 squares` on a floor whose budget affords ~3 creatures and whose sight disc is 7 means the exclusion zone is most of the map. The condition is unsatisfiable exactly where you need to rest — after breaking off — and only satisfiable in a corridor you had no reason to stand in.

**5. 186 turns of nothing, then a wipe instead of a run.** Lens #61: peak tension at 100%, flattest stretch 186 of 273 turns. Death is the *only* event; the curve has a stop, not an arc. And the packet's entire timeline is three lines — `restored 3 world(s), 1182 events`, a **718-second silence**, `wiped — 3 world(s) and every name discarded, back to one`. Twelve minutes between restoring the world and destroying it, with nothing recorded in between. That is the third reset in forty minutes and the second wipe (03:59 wipe at 6 turns, 04:01 begin-again, 04:38 wipe). The player's answer to dying was not another attempt. It was deleting the evidence.

## recommendations

**1. Smallest: separate the edge from its guard.** `src/core/tables.ts`, the `walkPath` placement from today's change. Keep the relic on the start→exit walk — the reach was right, "the door reaches you" is the correct instinct — but move the guard one room off it, so the sword is a *pickup* on the way and the fight is a *choice* you make armed. This answers the player's one sentence directly: the floor becomes passable, and passing it makes you stronger. Re-verify against the pinned door band (17→18 of 20) with `npm run balance`.

**2. Give the satchel a voice when it matters.** Not a number — a Chronicler line. Fire a journal sentence the first time health crosses below half **with a non-empty satchel**: the world noticing what your hand is holding. A player who dies at 0/10 carrying an unspent draught was never refusing the choice; they were never offered it. This is fiction doing a UI job, which is the cheapest kind of fix this game has.

**3. Forge candidate: make the rest rule able to fire.** Same trigger (hold still), same health condition, same words — *"The breath you do not spend comes back to you."* One number moves: `noCreatureWithin` **6 → 2**. At 2, resting becomes a real decision at the end of a retreat — break off, get two squares of air, buy a point back, decide whether to go again — instead of a condition that only holds where nothing was ever going to happen. It also finally gives M3 something to measure; a rule that has spoken zero times in four lives is currently costing the assay's credibility more than it costs the balance.

**4. The one big swing: a new floor on begin-again.** Re-seed depth 1 when a life ends, rather than replaying the same arrangement. Determinism belongs to *replay* — the chain, the golden run, the audit — not to the player's second attempt. As it stands, finding 3 is what the game teaches: try harder, walk further, get the identical ledger. Fresh geometry on each life turns three resets in forty minutes into three runs, and turns "very very very difficult to pass" into a problem the player believes they can solve differently rather than one they can only solve *again*.

## the shape of it

Not fun yet, and this run is unusually clear about why — the floor is not too hard, it is too *fixed*. Today's teaching-floor change was the right instinct aimed one square off: it reached toward the player with a sword and stationed a guard on it, so the fix that was meant to end "died bare" produced another death at might 3. But that is tuning, and tuning is cheap. The single absence that matters most is a **second attempt that differs from the first**. The player's 231 extra steps bought a byte-identical fight, and the 718 seconds of silence before the wipe is what a person does when they realize effort is not an input. Fix the guard's placement and the floor becomes passable; re-seed the floor and failure becomes worth having. Only the second one turns a wipe back into a run.
