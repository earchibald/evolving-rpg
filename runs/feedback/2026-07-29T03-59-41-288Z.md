# Six turns, nothing touched

> The player walked five squares, met nothing, lost no blood, and wiped the world before anything could happen to them.

*world main · ended playing at turn 6, depth 1 · submitted: wipe · 0 take(s), spoke: false*

## findings

- **The loudest thing in this run is that nothing was said.** No spoken words, no typed notes, across all 6 turns. Voice happens when something changes — a surprise, a hurt, a plan that worked. Six turns produced no change worth narrating, so the microphone recorded the honest answer: silence. This is not a player who was concentrating; it's a player with nothing to react to.

- **Turns 1–6: five steps, zero collisions, zero waits — the grid never pushed back.** Every step was legal, nothing blocked, and holding still was never worth doing. When all four directions cost the same and yield the same, movement isn't a decision, it's a cursor. The mechanism that fails here is *absence of asymmetry*: no move was better than any other move, so no move was a choice.

- **Two creatures were standing at the end and neither side ever swung.** 0 attacks made, 0 attacks received, over 6 turns. Threat existed on the board and never became threat in the fiction. The Lens of the Interest Curve is right for the wrong reason — tension "peaks at 0%" because tension never began. This is the core break: *time-to-first-consequence exceeded the player's patience*, and the patience was about six turns long.

- **Finished 10 of 10 hit points, might 3, speed 4, wits 3 — all of it untested.** Numbers the player never had to spend or defend are decoration. The Lens of Surprise says it plainly: nothing with a knowable probability has happened yet. A stat sheet that is never consulted teaches the player that the stat sheet doesn't matter.

- **The run ended by wipe at turn 6, at depth 1, full health, with the world exactly as it was born.** No rule fired. Nothing about this world was different from the default, and nothing in six turns hinted that it ever could be. The evolution premise made no visible promise inside the window the player gave it. A wipe at full HP is not a defeat report — it's a review.

## recommendations

1. **Smallest change — make creatures close.** In the creature movement/pursuit table, give the two starting creatures a pursuit step toward the player every turn from turn 1, at speed ≥ the player's. With 5 player steps and creatures walking in, first contact lands around turn 2–3 instead of never. This is one number in one table and it converts a walk into a chase.

2. **Shrink the first floor.** In the floor generation table for depth 1, cut the grid so that five steps cannot avoid an occupant. The player spent their whole attention budget crossing empty squares; the fix is fewer empty squares, not faster walking.

3. **Forge candidate — the world leans in when nothing is happening.** Sketched in the rule vocabulary:
   - *trigger*: turn ends
   - *condition*: three consecutive turns with no blow struck by or against the player
   - *effect*: the nearest creature moves one step toward the player and is named in the journal
   This gives dead air a mechanical consequence and — importantly — makes the *first* rule the player ever sees be one that creates events rather than tunes them. It also fires early enough to be observed inside a six-turn attention span.

4. **Fiction — name the thing on sight.** Add a first-sighting journal prompt: when a creature enters the player's view for the first time, one line that names it and says what it wants. Right now the player walked past two living things and the journal gave them no reason to care which direction to walk. A named creature is an object for a decision.

5. **Instrument the wipe.** The chain records that the world was wiped but not what the player was doing in the last turn before they gave up. Capture the wipe turn's board state in the witness packet — for runs that end in abandonment, that final frame is the most diagnostic thing in the file, and right now it's the one thing missing.

## the shape of it

Not fun yet, and not close — but the failure is clean and single-caused, which is the good kind. Nothing in this run is badly tuned; things are merely *far apart*. The player and the danger occupied the same floor for six turns and never met, so every other system — combat, stats, rules, surprise, the interest curve — sat idle waiting for a contact that the geometry never delivered. The single absence that matters most next is **first contact inside three turns**. Until the player has been hit once, or has hit something once, there is no consequence to feel, nothing for the lenses to measure, no material for a rule to evolve against, and nothing for the microphone to hear.
