## recommendations

**1. The tuning is half-landed — finish it one floor deeper.** `spawnBudget` in `src/core/tables.ts` now pays depth 1 the FULL `stretch` and spends it only on kinds at or under `DOOR_PRICE_CAP = 15`, and the comment quotes this exact complaint. Good — that is the right table and the right lever. But this run died on **depth 2**, where the factor drops back to `bountyStretch` (1.5 on the expanse, half the stretch). Check that depth 2 on a stretched board fields more than three bodies before declaring boredom fixed; the player's sentence was about floor one, but their run was bored on floor two as well.

**2. Let the journal name the emptiness.** 445 turns of nothing said is the cheapest thing here to cure, and it costs no balance. Give the journal narration prompt a line for the flat stretch — the corridor going on too long, the floor noticing it has nothing to offer. Dead air currently reads as a defect; narrated, it reads as pacing, and it buys time for the density fix to matter.

**3. Forge candidate — the floor notices you are alone.** Makes emptiness self-correcting rather than something the spawn table has to predict.
- trigger: 30 turns pass with no creature in sight
- condition: depth ≥ 1
- effect: spawn one level-appropriate wanderer at the far edge of sight
Emptiness becomes a wind-up rather than a void, and no floor can be crossed unmolested twice.

**4. Forge candidate — give a wound an ending.** 24 damage that produces no corpse teaches that hitting is pointless.
- trigger: a creature is struck below a third of its hit points
- condition: none
- effect: it breaks — flees toward the stairs, or calls
Either outcome is closure: the player finished something, or the floor answered. Both beat the current third option, which is that the exchange simply stops mattering.

**5. The one big swing — give the floor something it wants from you.** 1174 steps, 5 pickups, 60 walls, and no destination but down. The warden already guards the stairs at depth 3; pull an equivalent object of desire to depths 1–2 — a guarded pantry, a thing worth the risk of the room it sits in. Right now descent is the only verb the floor rewards, so the player optimises for walking, and walking is what bored them.

## the shape of it

The machinery under this run is more careful than the run is fun — threat is priced, verbs are costed, the door has a price cap, and the designer's own boredom is already quoted in the source. But the run itself contains no completed transaction of any kind: nothing died, nothing was defended, no fight was chosen, no line was spoken in character, and the world was deleted to file the complaint. The single absence that matters most is not more monsters. It is **one body on the floor** — one fight the player chose to finish and did, so the game can finally show what its reward feels like. Density is the setup for that moment; closure is the moment. Ship the closure, or the fuller floor just means being bored more often.
