## recommendations

**1. (smallest) Make depth-1 attackers land.** 0-for-4 is not variance, it is a table. Raise monster to-hit (or lower the player's effective defense) at depth 1 so a four-swing exchange expects ~2 hits and costs 2–4 HP. Name: the creature to-hit / damage table for depth 1. The goal is not difficulty — it is making the HP readout carry information for the first time.

**2. Raise encounter density so 127 steps buys more than one fight.** One creature engaged per 127 steps is roughly one meaningful event per two minutes of walking. Target 4–6 encounters per 128 turns at depth 1. Name: spawns-per-room / rooms-per-floor in level generation. This alone should halve the 59-turn flat.

**3. Forge candidate — the dead-air breaker.** Sketched in rule vocabulary:
- *trigger:* `turns_since_state_change >= 12`
- *condition:* `no creature in sight AND depth > 0`
- *effect:* spawn one creature at the edge of sight, or reveal the nearest unexplored exit
Cheap, self-limiting, and it makes the flattest stretch structurally impossible. It also has the side benefit of guaranteeing at least one rule fires per run, so the world is seen to be a world with rules in it.

**4. Give the pickup a line and a number.** Any item taken must either move a stat or print one sentence of its own. Name: the pickup/acquisition prompt. A player who never learns what an item did will stop bending down.

**5. (the one big swing) Seed every new world with at least one live rule at birth.** "The world plays as it was born" currently means "the world plays as an empty grid". Birth each world with 1–2 starting rules whose effects are visible inside the first 30 turns, so the player's first question is *what is this world like?* rather than *is there anything here?* Everything else on this list makes the run less flat; this is the one that makes it a run in *this* game.
