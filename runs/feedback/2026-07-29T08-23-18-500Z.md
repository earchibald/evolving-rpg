## findings

- **Turn 1 is the whole run, and it contains no decision.** The chain records 0 steps, 0 held-still turns, 0 swings, 0 pickups — the player arrived, looked, and left for another world. The only choice the world offered on its opening frame was whether to keep playing, and the mechanism that failed is *no opening pressure*: 3 creatures were standing, none of them close enough or active enough to convert "look at the board" into "do something about the board."

- **The silence is total, and it is the loudest signal here.** "The player said nothing aloud this run" and "they typed nothing." A player narrates when the world diverges from what they expected — a surprise, a loss, a rule doing something strange. Zero words across the entire session means nothing crossed the threshold of worth-remarking-on. This is not a player who was frustrated; it is a player who was not yet engaged enough to be frustrated.

- **The evolving part of the evolving RPG was invisible at the only moment the player was watching.** "No rule fired during this run" and "RULES IN FORCE: none — the world plays as it was born." Whatever makes this game different from a grid with monsters on it had not yet had a chance to introduce itself by the time the player made their exit decision. - **Nothing was at stake, and the numbers prove it.** Final state: 10 of 10 hit points, might 3, speed 4, wits 3 — identical to birth. 0 damage taken, 0 dealt, 0 creatures died, 3 still standing. There was no consequence to feel because no consequence was possible in the span of one turn. A run that cannot change the player's numbers cannot change the player's mind.

- **The lenses are blind to this failure mode.** "WHAT THE LENSES MEASURED: (no readings)." The instrument produced nothing for a run that ended in abandonment — meaning the measuring apparatus currently cannot distinguish "played and found dull" from "not played." The most common early-game failure is the walk-away, and it is exactly the one the lenses do not see.

## recommendations

1. **Make the walk-away legible (smallest).** On submission, have the witness packet always emit at least one reading: turns played, distance to nearest creature at turn 1, and the submit reason. A run that ends at turn 1 with reason "left for another world" should arrive as a *measurement*, not as five sections of "(none)". This is instrumentation only — no game change — and without it every future abandoned run reads as identical to this one.

2. **Tune the opening spawn distance.** The board-generation spawn table places creatures far enough that turn 1 has no contact and no threat. Pull the nearest-creature distance down so at least one of the three is within a step or two of the player's start. Cost is one number; effect is that the first frame poses a question instead of presenting a diorama.

3. **Forge candidate — the world notices you.** In rule vocabulary:
   - *trigger:* on turn start
   - *condition:* turn ≤ 3 AND player has taken no action this run
   - *effect:* nearest creature advances one tile toward the player, and the journal names it
   
   This makes the world the one that moves first when the player is standing still, and — more importantly — it means a rule *fires* inside the window where the player is still deciding whether to stay. The premise gets to speak before the exit.

4. **Fiction: the arrival line.** Add a first-turn journal prompt that names one specific, concrete thing about *this* world at depth 1 — not "you descend," but a detail unique to world-2's generation. One sentence, delivered before the player's first input. It costs nothing mechanically and gives the silence something to push against.

## the shape of it

This run is not evidence that the game is unfun; it is evidence that the game has not yet had a first impression. Nothing measured, nothing said, nothing risked — the player stood in the doorway of world-2 and closed it again inside a single turn. The single absence that matters most next is **an opening turn that costs something to ignore**: until the first frame contains a creature close enough to matter or a rule visibly doing something, every judgment about whether this is getting more fun is being made about a world the player never actually entered. Fix the door before tuning the rooms.
