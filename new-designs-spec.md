Technical Design Specification: Economy, Mining, and Core EnhancementsTarget Audience: Primary Coding AgentTarget Repository: earchibald/evolving-rpgI. Base Product Recommendations (In-Place Enhancements)These recommendations build upon the existing highly modular TypeScript architecture (specifically leveraging core, canon, and critic modules) to deepen the gameplay loop.Combat Stance Combos (core/volley-stance.ts & core/dual-wield.ts):Mechanic: Introduce "Momentum." If a player switches from a defensive stance to an offensive stance immediately following a successful dodge, grant a guaranteed critical hit or a free off-hand attack.Implementation: Store a lastTurnAction state and evaluate it during the stance-switch execution phase.Environmental Synergies (core/grid.ts & core/traps.ts):Mechanic: Allow forced movement to trigger traps. Give the player a push/knockback ability in specific combat stances to shove enemies into existing traps.Implementation: Modify the trap trigger listener to accept non-player entities and apply faction-agnostic damage/effects.Encumbrance Penalties (play/store.ts & core/satchel.ts):Mechanic: Instead of hard inventory limits, implement immediate combat consequences for hoarding. A full satchel degrades the player's dodge stat or locks out agile combat stances.Implementation: Add a calculated weight getter to the inventory state that hooks into the combat modifier calculations.Diegetic AI Critic (critic/critic.ts):Mechanic: Treat the Critic as a mischievous "Audience" that actively judges the run. Boring play (repetitive actions) spawns negative modifiers (e.g., "enemies explode on death"). Stylish play spawns buffs or rare loot.Implementation: Map the Critic's evaluation outputs directly to mapgen or entity-spawning event listeners.II. Summary of New MechanicsWe are introducing a secondary "Push-Your-Luck" gameplay loop seamlessly integrated with the main dungeon crawler. This includes a numerical Gold economy, a state-stacked subterranean Mining Level, a durability-based resource gathering system, localized crafting, and a strict 5-slot Scroll Belt to act as a high-value gold sink in the main dungeon. Crucially, the mining loop features undetectable traps that can severely penalize greedy players by corrupting their resources, draining their tool durability, or spawning monsters in the suspended main-level state.III. Detailed Implementation Specs1. Economy & ValuationCore Item Interface: Add a baseValue: number property to all items.Gold System: Implement Gold as a purely numerical UI state variable, explicitly distinct from the physical satchel array to preserve inventory space.Balancing: Base dungeon loot should sell for nominal amounts (1-2G). Significant wealth generation is strictly gated behind the Mining Level.2. State Management & The Mining LevelThe Trigger: Inject a SuspiciousWall entity into core/mapgen.ts with a low spawn probability (e.g., 5% per room).State Stack:Enter: When interacting with the wall, push the current dungeon state (grid, entities, player coordinates) to a stack. Generate the temporary Mining Level state.Exit: Pop the state stack, returning the player to the exact coordinates and grid state they left.Mining Entities: Populate the level with mineable blocks: Rock (70%), Iron Vein (20%), Gold Vein (9%), Diamond Vein (1%).3. The Mining Shop & CraftingPlacement: Hardcode to spawn at the entry coordinates of the Mining Level.Inventory: Sells Wooden Pickaxe (e.g., 50G) and provides a UI to sell mined ores.Durability: Pickaxes require a usesLeft state. A wooden pickaxe degrades by 1 per mined block (breaks at 0).In-Store Crafting Table:Recipe: Wooden Pickaxe + 3x Iron Ore = Iron Pickaxe.Iron Pickaxe Specs: Higher durability (e.g., 40 uses) and a passive buff allowing the player to crack base Rock in one turn instead of two.4. The Scroll Belt & Main Dungeon MerchantMain Dungeon Merchant: An occasionally spawning NPC in standard levels that sells randomized buffs, healing, and Scrolls.Scroll Belt Array:Create a dedicated array (e.g., activeScrolls: Scroll[]) strictly capped at length === 5.Rotation: Implement a command to rotate the array (e.g., belt.push(belt.shift())). The UI must always display activeScrolls[0] as the "Ready" spell.Execution: Firing the cast command triggers the effect of activeScrolls[0], splices it from the array, and updates the UI.5. Undetectable Mining TrapsAssign a hidden trapType property to 5-10% of generated Rock and Ore entities. These execute immediately upon the Mine verb resolving, prior to loot dropping.Immediate Traps (Friction):Toxic Gas: Deals flat percentage damage to the player and applies a "Coughing" debuff (reduces accuracy for $X$ turns).Seismic Shift: Destroys $1d4$ adjacent unmined blocks (converting them to empty floor or worthless rubble), destroying potential wealth.Brittle Rock: Instantly reduces the equipped pickaxe's usesLeft by 50%.Delayed Traps (Main Level Consequences):The Deep Echo:Flavor Text: "A deep, echoing crack reverberates through the ceiling above..."Execution: The engine accesses the suspended state in the stack (the main dungeon level). It injects $1d3$ hostile entities into the room the player originally departed from.Result: Upon exiting the Mining Level (popping the state), the player is immediately ambushed in a room they previously believed to be safe.IV. State-Based Sprite SwappingAnimation/Direction: Ensure the SpriteManager listens to entity states. For example, switch the sx/sy coordinates to the "attack" sprite or flip the image context horizontally if the entity is moving left.Mining State: The SuspiciousWall, Iron Vein, and Diamond Vein entities must share the bounding box rules of standard wall tiles but possess unique sprite lookups.V. Sprite Generation Methodology & PromptingAs the game evolves, new entities, items, and biomes will be added. To maintain visual consistency with the existing sprite sheet, anyone (or any AI) generating new assets must adhere strictly to the following methodology.1. Art Style DefinitionFormat: 16-bit era pixel art.Perspective: Top-down orthographic RPG (slightly angled so character faces and item details are clearly visible, resembling classic SNES/GBA RPGs).Grid Constraint: Designs must fit within a square aspect ratio (ideally natively designed in a 16x16 or 32x32 pixel grid, then upscaled cleanly).Outlines: All characters, monsters, and items must have a thick, solid dark outline (usually dark gray or black). This is critical for visual separation against dark dungeon floor tiles.Palette: Earthy base tones (grays, browns, deep greens) with vibrant, high-saturation accents for magic, gems, and important items (bright blues, glowing yellows, crimson).2. The Core Prompting FormulaWhen utilizing an image generation model to create new assets, use the following base prompt template to enforce consistency:Template:"A 16-bit pixel art sprite of [SUBJECT/ENTITY], classic top-down RPG style. The sprite must have a thick dark outline and be isolated on a dark gray checkerboard background. Earthy color palette with [SPECIFIC COLOR] accents. Clean pixels, no blurring, centered in a square frame."Examples:New Boss: "A 16-bit pixel art sprite of a massive Minotaur wielding an axe, classic top-down RPG style. The sprite must have a thick dark outline and be isolated on a dark gray checkerboard background. Earthy brown color palette with bright red accents on the axe. Clean pixels, no blurring, centered in a square frame."New Material: "A 16-bit pixel art sprite of a glowing Uranium Ore rock, classic top-down RPG style. The sprite must have a thick dark outline and be isolated on a dark gray checkerboard background. Dark stone palette with neon green accents. Clean pixels, no blurring."3. Iteration and Cleanup (For the Agent/Developer)Background Removal: Generated images will often include the requested checkerboard or solid background. The developer must use a simple color-keying script (or manual removal) to set the background to alpha (0,0,0,0) before appending it to the master sprite sheet.Grid Snapping: If the generated asset does not perfectly align with the 16x16 scale, it must be downsampled nearest-neighbor (without anti-aliasing) to fit the engine's sprite dictionary constraints.
